# Round 5: can MVSep's Mega 53 (MIT weights) replace the Demucs models for all six stems?

Date: 2026-11-24. htdemucs and htdemucs_6s weights are "for scientific purposes only" (round 1). Mega 53's are MIT. This round scores Mega 53's heads on every stem Fretboard's mixer plays (vocals, drums, bass, guitar, piano/keys, other) against today's Demucs-based recipes, on the 26 Cambridge-MT remixes of round 4, and gathers what each recipe costs. All separations ran on the Linux box (i5-12450H, RTX 4060), Mega on CUDA in fp32 like round 4.

Full tables (pooled, per set, per item, every head choice): [tables.md](tables.md), generated by `r5_summary.py` from [scores/](scores/) (one JSON per item).

**Yes: Kim + Mega replaces both Demucs models without losing on any stem we can measure fairly.**

- Kim's vocals;

**Per stem:**

1. **Vocals: keep Kim.**
   - Mega's `vocal` head on the mix is +2.03 dB over `hyb6` (26/26), but 0.19 dB under Kim [−0.46, +0.19]. Kim is better on 21/26 songs.
   - This matches the user's listening on Avalon and Butterfly Effect: Kim 12.81 / 13.96 dB, Mega 12.56 / 13.02.
   - Mega's vocals are clearly better on a few songs: JetB 12.69 vs 8.99 for Kim, Man About A Dog 9.51 vs 8.79. Neither model is always right.
   - Mega's `lead-vocal` + `back-vocal` heads, summed, are unusable: −8.4 dB vs `hyb6`, 0/26. The sum plays at roughly the level of the whole mix.
2. **Guitar: Mega, confirming round 4.**
   - +2.65 dB vs `hyb6` and +2.16 dB vs the chain, 26/26 for both.
   - The scores are round 4's again: `mega_inst` 6.92, `6s_inst` 4.76.
3. **Bass: Mega.** +0.92 dB vs `hyb6` (20/26) and +0.78 dB vs the chain.
   - It loses on a few songs: Juanita Dientes Verdes −1.2, Man About A Dog −0.2.
   - It wins on more, and by more: Juliet's Rescue +5.1, Neon Hornet +2.9.
4. **Piano / keys: Mega's `keys` head.**
   - Demucs has practically no keys stem on these songs: 0.7 dB uSDR.
   - Mega's `keys` head gets 5.24, better on 10/10 songs. It contains what the `piano` head finds: within 0.1 dB of it on Angels, Avalon and JetB, and only Al James is worse (2.78 vs 3.47).
   - It also catches organ and e-piano that the `piano` head misses: Impossible Colours 0.4 → 8.4, Man About A Dog 0.1 → 6.7.
   - Summing `piano` + `keys` counts the piano twice (2.09). Summing all five keyboard heads is worse still (−0.75).
   - On songs without keys, the `keys` head plays at −61 dB re mix (inaudible), against −67 for `piano`.
5. **Drums: a tie with the chain, and better than `hyb6` on 20/26.**
   - +0.73 dB on the 18 non-MUSDB excerpts (16/18), but −0.51 on the 8 MUSDB test songs (4/8).
   - Two songs carry the loss: Enda Reilly 16.0 → 10.6 dB and Angels In Amplifiers 8.6 → 5.8. Their drum groups include brushes and a percussion track.
   - Mega's separate `percussion`, `tambourine` and `congas` heads are **not** the cause (tested 2026-11-25, `r5_drums.sh` → `scores/drums.json`; heads on Kim's instrumental, CUDA fp32 as above):
     - `drums` + `percussion` + `tambourine` + `congas` vs the `drums` head alone: −0.10 [−0.38, +0.12], 13/26 up. It helps where a percussion track exists (Enda Reilly 10.6 → 11.5, Al James 8.4 → 9.6) but costs Butterfly Effect 8.5 → 5.7, and Angels stays at 5.7 (chain 8.8).
     - The kit-piece heads (`kick` + `snare` + `toms` + `hh` + the three percussion heads) in place of `drums`: −3.03 [−3.54, −2.55], 0/26. The `drums` head is far better than its parts.
     - So the `drums` head alone stays in the recipe. The two MUSDB losses are something else (brushes, or how these mixes differ from Mega's training data); untested.
6. **Other: no measurable difference, and the comparison is unfair to Mega.**
   - Mega has no "other" head, so other = mix − the five stems. That makes it carry every error of the other five.
   - Demucs' other built the same way loses as well: `hyb6_fix` is −0.63 dB uSDR and −1.76 "removed" against `hyb6`'s own other.
   - Among residual others, Mega's is as good as Demucs' or better: removed 19.25 for `kim_mega_keys` vs 18.78 for `chain_fix` and 18.18 for `hyb6_fix`.
   - It is worse than Demucs' own model-made other, mostly on songs with no "other" parts. There it plays at −21.2 dB re mix (`kim_mega_keys`) vs −23.0 for the chain.
   - Only 9 songs have other parts, so the other uSDR CIs span ±1.5–2 dB.
   - Fretboard's mixer already builds "other" as a residual, so it already pays this cost today.

**Mega only (`mega_mix_keys`, no Kim):**
- Within 0.2 dB of `kim_mega_keys` on drums, bass and guitar; keys 0.26 dB lower.
- Vocals 0.19 dB below Kim's, but still +2.03 dB over `hyb6`.
- Against `hyb6`: drums +0.27 [−0.28, +0.69] 22/26, bass +0.80, guitar +2.49, keys +4.26.
- It is the replacement to use if Kim's 457 MB and its CPU time are too much.

**Sum to the mix:**
- `hyb6` and the chain leave −27.0 and −27.7 dB re mix (worst items −22.4 and −21.3).
- The Mega recipes are exact, by construction.

**Cost** (details under *Cost*):
- Mega's heads are almost free: 5 heads cost about what 1 does.
- **GPU (RTX 4060, fp32), measured:**
  - Mega with 5 heads takes 0.36 s per audio second (1 head: 0.35).
  - A 3.5 min song took 89 s wall including the model load, with 2.0 GB of GPU memory.
- **x86 CPU at 1 thread, estimated** (Rust; the box measurements are still to come):
  - `kim_mega_keys` ≈ 20–21 CPU-s per audio second;
  - the chain ≈ 11–12;
  - `hyb6` ≈ 2.
  - So the Demucs-free recipe costs **about 1.8× the chain's CPU**. The extra is Mega's backbone, about 10.5 CPU-s per audio second.
- **Mac (M1 Pro, 1 thread, shared machine), measured:** Rust `hyb6` 0.65, chain 3.74 and Kim 3.10 CPU-s per audio-s on a 30 s excerpt. PyTorch Mega with 5 heads 9.7 long-track, the same as 1 head.

## Data

The 26 Cambridge-MT remixes of round 4 ([../round4/round4.md](../round4/round4.md), `r4_mixcmt.py`): `cmt8` (8 MUSDB test songs, 90 s each) and `cmtx` (18 non-MUSDB rock excerpts, 18–39 s), 20.2 min in all. Each item stores mixture, vocals, drums, bass, guitar and, where the song has them, keys and other. Every mixture is a plain sum of its parts:

- **"other" is stored only when the song has such parts** (9 of 26 items; banjo, mandolin, strings, synth pads, FX and the like). Where it is missing it is silence. I score other against mix − vocals − drums − bass − guitar − keys; that equals `other.wav` (or silence) within **−145 dB** of the mix on every item (`parts_sum_err_db` in each score JSON).
- **keys** exist on 10 of 26 items (cmt8: Angels In Amplifiers, Ben Carrigan, Al James, Signe Jakobsen; cmtx: Avalon, Impossible Colours, JetB, Man About A Dog, Peter White, StreetNoise). The recipes' "piano" stem is scored against this keys group (piano, organ, synths, e-pianos by file name).
- A stem's **uSDR** is only defined on items that have it. "Stem removed" uSDR is defined on all 26.

**Mega inference.**
- The method is round 4's (`r4_mega.py`): MSST's generic `demix`, 20 s chunks, overlap 2, fp32.
- It runs in a copy, `r5_mega.py`, that builds only the heads asked for and memory-maps the checkpoint. With 11 heads that peaks at 4.2 GB of RSS on the CPU. Round 4's loader built all 53 heads first and needed 7.8 GB or more.

**Checks.**
- **The box set reproduces the earlier Mac set.** Six cmtx items were first separated on the Mac (Mega on the M1 Pro's CPU; Demucs and Kim through an Accelerate build of htdemucs-rs). ## Metrics (`r5_score.py`)

As round 4, per stem:
- **uSDR** = 10·log10(Σs² / Σ(s − ŝ)²) over the item, both channels.
- **stem removed** = what the mixer's mute plays, mix − ŝ, against the truth mix − s: 10·log10(Σ(mix − s)² / Σ(s − ŝ)²). Within an item it ranks variants exactly as uSDR does; it is also defined on items without the part (there it measures how loudly a stem that should be silent plays).
- **level on items without the part**: 10·log10(Σŝ² / Σmix²).
- **sum-to-mix residual** per recipe: 10·log10(Σ(mix − Σ stems)² / Σmix²).
- **Statistics:** paired per item against `hyb6`; brackets are 95 % bootstrap intervals over items (2000 resamples, seed 0); k/n = items improved.

## Caveats

- **Training-data overlap.**
  - Mega 53's training data is undisclosed. Its trainer says he doesn't hold the rights to all of it, so Cambridge-MT, and so all 26 of these songs, may be in it.
  - Kim's training data includes MUSDB18 (round 1). `cmt8` is 8 MUSDB test songs, remixed from the Cambridge-MT multitracks rather than MUSDB's mixes.
  - htdemucs trained on MUSDB18-HQ train plus 800 extra songs, without MUSDB test (round 4). htdemucs_6s trained on MUSDB18-HQ plus Meta's internal set; which MUSDB split it used is not stated.
  - So every model may have seen some of these recordings; Mega and Kim are the likelier ones.
  - Kim's lead over `hyb6` on vocals is larger on the MUSDB songs (+3.10 dB, 8/8) than on the non-MUSDB excerpts (+1.83 dB, 16/18), which is what overlap would produce. The non-MUSDB excerpts are the safer evidence.
  - The two sets disagree in one place: Mega's drums are better on the non-MUSDB excerpts and worse on the MUSDB songs. Overlap favouring Mega would have predicted the opposite, so overlap does not explain that difference.
- **Residual "other".**
  - Mega's other is mix − five stems, so it holds every stem's error.
  - On songs with no "other" parts (17 of 26), it plays leaked instruments 2–4 dB louder than Demucs' own other (−21.2 / −19.4 dB re mix for `kim_mega_keys` / `mega_mix`, vs −23.0 for the chain).
  - Judge it against the same construction for Demucs (`hyb6_fix`, `chain_fix`), where Mega is equal or better. Against a model-made other it looks worse than it is.
  - Only 9 songs have true other parts, so the other uSDR has CIs of ±1.5–2 dB.
- **Keys.** Ten songs have keys. Their keys group mixes piano, organ, synth and e-piano by file name, which is why Mega's broad `keys` head fits it better than its `piano` head. On a pure-piano song the `piano` head may be the better choice (Al James: 3.47 vs 2.78).
- **Unity remixes.** These are unity-gain sums with no EQ, compression or reverb, drier than released mixes. Absolute dB will differ on commercial songs; the ranking is what carries over (round 4).
- **Short items.** `cmtx` excerpts are 18–39 s, and `cmt8` is 90 s of each song.
- **Cost.**
  - The x86 CPU figures per recipe are estimates from separately measured parts. The direct box runs are still to come.
  - The Mac timings ran on a shared machine (another agent was using it). Treat them as indicative and the CPU time as the more reliable number.

## Recommendation

1. **Replace both Demucs models with `kim_mega_keys`.** That is Kim's vocals, then Mega 53's `drums`, `bass`, `guitar` and `keys` heads on Kim's instrumental, with other = mix − the five.
   - It is better than today's candidate `hyb6` on vocals (+2.22), bass (+0.92), guitar (+2.65) and keys (+4.51), with CIs clear of zero. It is level on drums (+0.35, CI includes 0) and level on other.
   - Against the chain, it is level on vocals, drums and other, and better on bass, guitar and keys.
   - Every model in it has MIT weights. No "scientific purposes only" weights are left.
   - The stems sum to the mix exactly.
2. **If Kim's 457 MB and its ≈ 10 CPU-s per audio-s are too much, use `mega_mix_keys`** (Mega only, five heads on the mix).
   - Vocals are 0.19 dB below Kim; drums, bass and guitar are within 0.2 dB of `kim_mega_keys`, keys 0.26 dB below.
   - It is still +2.0 dB over `hyb6` on vocals.
   - ≈ 10.7 CPU-s per audio-s, 177 MB.
3. **Don't use:**
   - `lead-vocal` + `back-vocal` (−8.4 dB);
   - `piano` + `keys` summed (double-counts piano);
   - all five keyboard heads summed;
   - the residual other built from the chain plus Mega's guitar (`chain+mg`: other −4.6 dB removed). Mixing models makes the residual worse.
4. **Before shipping:**
   - ~~Check the drum loss on brushes / percussion~~: done, adding percussion heads does not help (item 5).
   - Do a blind listen of the "other" stem on two or three songs without "other" parts.
   - Re-time on x86.
